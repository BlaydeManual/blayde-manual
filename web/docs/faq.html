The Basics

What is this, actually?

Old manuals for anything you own, such as a motorcycle, a boat, a plane, a tractor, a home appliance, a sewing machine, or a ham radio, often have terrible photos: low-resolution scans that get worse every time someone re-scans them. This project replaces those photos with real ones, taken by real owners doing the actual work, and patches them back into your own copy of the manual.

Is this legal? Manuals are copyrighted, right?

Yes, they are, and that shaped almost every decision here. The public part of this project never contains the manual's own photos or text, only structure (page numbers, where a photo belongs). Photos are contributed by real people, licensed by them, and patched into your own copy of a manual you already own. Nothing copyrighted ever gets redistributed.

Hasn't someone already done this?

Not quite. Paid services exist that shoot their own manual photos professionally (Cyclepedia is the closest example, for motorcycles), but they're closed, centralized, and usually scoped to one category at most. Nobody's built the open, crowd-sourced, legally-clean version that works the same way across a garage, a marina, a hangar, a farm, a home, or a hobby bench. That's the actual gap this fills.

Was AI used in making this?

Yes, openly. This project was built with Claude (an AI assistant) doing the coding, research, and drafting, guided by a human who made every real call: what to build, which license to use, which tradeoffs to accept, and what to reject or correct along the way. Nothing about that is hidden. There's a full public ledger of it (ledgers/TheBlayde_AILedger.md), including the places the human caught real mistakes the AI made. If other contributors keep their own ledgers of their AI-assisted work someday, that's the folder where they'd live too, one per contributor.

Is AI doing something when it patches my manual?

No, and that's on purpose. Building the code used AI (see above); running it doesn't. Patching a manual is plain, predictable software: it reads pixel patterns to find where a photo goes, reads text off the page (OCR, the same kind of tech scanners have used for years) to label it, and swaps the image in. No AI model, no server, no query goes out while it works. That's why it can run entirely in your browser with nothing uploaded, and why it'll still work exactly the same way five years from now.

Your Photos

If I contribute a photo, do I still own it?

Yes, always, no matter where a copy of it ends up. Contributing a photo licenses it (CC-BY 4.0: anyone can use it, they just have to credit you). It doesn't transfer ownership to anyone: not to Blayde Manual, not to anyone else, not ever.

Can I revoke a photo I contributed?

Not retroactively, and that's by design, not an oversight. CC-BY 4.0 is deliberately irrevocable once granted. If it could be pulled back at will, nobody could safely build on it, and every manual already patched with your photo would be sitting on legally shaky ground. What you can do: ask a maintainer to take it down, and it stops being offered to anyone from that point forward. That request itself is still a direct ask today, not yet a one-click automated flow, but the removal it leads to is completely real. It just can't reach back into copies other people already generated before the removal.

Do I get credit?

Yes, and visibly, not buried somewhere only a developer would look. Every photo you contribute gets a small "photo: @you" credit printed directly under it, every time someone enhances their own legal copy with the Blayde Manual overlay.

Where do photos physically live?

As ordinary image files stored alongside that item's manual, nothing fancier than that. No separate photo service, no database. See the diagram below.

Does my photo carry my phone's GPS location or other hidden data?

No. Cameras and phones embed a surprising amount into a photo file beyond the picture itself: GPS coordinates, camera make and model, timestamps, and editing history. None of that is published. Your photo is stripped down to just the pixels before it's submitted, and checked again automatically before it can be merged. If either check finds anything beyond the image itself, the photo is rejected, not silently cleaned and published anyway.

Contributing

Do I have to share my photos publicly?

No, not if you save it as a draft instead of submitting it. A draft stays entirely on this device until you choose to submit it: nothing leaves your browser, and no account is needed. Signing in only happens at that later step, never before. Patching your own manual with already-approved photos works the same way, fully on your device, no account needed at all. The one thing not built yet: taking a saved draft to a different device without an account.

What if I lose track of what I've already photographed?

That's a real, planned feature: when you're working through several photos in one session, the app shows a live checklist of everything in that section (what's done, what's still needed), so you're never left wondering "wait, did I already do that one?"

Do I need a computer, or can I do this all from my phone?

The goal is phone-only, camera-in-hand, no download required, same spirit as those "flash a device from your browser" tools. That piece is still being designed, but it's a core goal, not an afterthought.

Who's In Charge

Does a small team have to approve every single photo?

No. That wouldn't scale, and it's not how it works. A small Blayde Manual team reviews new submissions: is the source real, does the indexing look right, did someone give it an honest pass. That's true whether it's a brand-new item or another manual joining one that already exists, in any category. Day-to-day photo review is never Blayde Manual's job. That's always each item's own dedicated group of maintainers, at whatever pace that community sets.

Does approval need everyone to agree?

No. Adding a new item just needs one Blayde Manual team member to review and approve it, not unanimous agreement from all five, and not always the same one, which keeps things moving without anyone becoming a bottleneck. Day-to-day photo approval on an item is different: it takes two of that item's own maintainers to agree, since that's an ongoing job rather than a one-time decision.

What if my item has more than one manual, like a factory manual and a Haynes manual?

Both can exist as part of the same item, maintained by the same team, since they're different books about the identical item, so the community reviewing them is the same community. The two scans don't share photo coordinates, but that's just kept separate within the one item, not treated as two separate items.

That's different from a real, different version of the same model. For a vehicle, that means model years: an SV650 from 1999 and one from 2005 might share a name, but they're mechanically different bikes (different parts, different procedures), not two books about the same one. For a Home or Hobby item, there's usually no "model year" at all. The equivalent is a real manufacturer variant code when one exists (a Singer 15-91 is a genuinely different sewing machine from a plain Singer 15), or nothing at all when the brand and model are already unique on their own (a Yaesu FT-101 doesn't need anything else added). Either way, a real different version becomes its own separate item, with its own maintainer team, the same as any two unrelated items would.

If I submit a brand new item, does it belong to me?

No, not even briefly. The moment you submit, it's created under Blayde Manual's own account, kept private so nobody but a team member can see it yet. Once a Blayde Manual team member reviews and approves, the manual goes public and you're the first real maintainer! This comes with the same privileges any later maintainer has. Invite your friends! That's deliberate: the project's survival shouldn't depend on any one person's account staying active forever.

For Maintainers

What's actually expected of me as a maintainer?

One thing: look at a submitted photo and decide if it's a good, honest, in-focus contribution for that procedure. That's the whole job.

A short list of things that are explicitly not on you, so you never end up worrying about them: how big the project gets, scaling to more contributors or more items overall, whether the project succeeds, and the legal/copyright architecture (already solved once, at the project level). All of that is the Blayde Manual team's concern, not a maintainer's.

What if I go quiet, or can't keep up?

Try to find someone to hand things off to if you're stepping back. If you can't, don't worry about it. An item with no active maintainer still works exactly the same for anyone patching their own copy, no matter what, and that part's already true today. A visible "passive" label, so contributors can see at a glance an item could use a new maintainer, is a real planned feature, not built yet.

How many maintainers should an item have?

Somewhere around 2-5 active ones is the healthy range. One is a single point of failure; past five or so, ownership starts feeling like nobody's job in particular, and a new volunteer is usually more useful helping an understaffed item than stacking onto one that already has plenty of coverage.

The Business Side

Is this free?

Yes, and that's not just a nice idea. It's the reason contributing works at all. Nobody's going to photograph their own brake job for a paywall. The free, open version is what makes the whole thing possible.

What do I actually need to get started? Do I have to sign up for anything?

Depends what you're doing. To just use Blayde Manual, patching your own legally-owned PDF with whatever real photos the community has already approved, you don't need an account of any kind. Everything is public and readable by anyone.

To contribute a photo, yes, you do need a free GitHub account, since that's how your credit and your license grant are attached to what you submit. There's no cost involved either way, GitHub accounts are free, but contributing does require that one piece of setup that just using it doesn't.

What stops a big company from just taking this and selling it?

They could legally reuse credited photos (that's what an open license allows, on purpose), but they can't use the Blayde Manual name or take over the actual living community, and a static copy goes stale the moment it's copied, since it stops getting new contributions. The code itself is also licensed (AGPL) specifically so a company can't quietly turn it into a closed paid product without giving their changes back.

Could this ever make money?

If it ever does, it's more likely to look like affiliate links to real parts and tools next to a procedure (the way iFixit works) than a paywall on the manual itself. The free core stays free either way.

Scaling to More Items

Won't storing photos for hundreds of items get expensive or slow?

No. Each item is stored separately and sized independently, not lumped into one giant shared pool. Storage limits apply per item, not across the whole project, so adding a 500th item never makes an existing one closer to any kind of limit.

How do you tell items apart? Is there some official database?

No official database, on purpose. A rigid database would risk excluding exactly the older, rarer items this project is for. Brand, model, and (for a vehicle) year range are taken from what the manual itself says, with a real person checking it makes sense before it's approved. A Home or Hobby item usually doesn't have a "year range" at all, so that part just doesn't apply, but the same person-checks-it-makes-sense process still does.

See README.md for the technical pipeline, LEGAL.md for the full legal reasoning, and ROADMAP.md for what's designed but not yet built.
